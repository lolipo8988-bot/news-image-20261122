## The day's stories

**Lead stories**

1. **Gulf war widens** — Fire at a civilian plant in Bahrain after an Iranian
   strike; no injuries. Saudi defences down a Houthi missile and a
   Riyadh-bound drone. Trump threatens to sink tankers testing the US
   blockade; Iran downs a fourth US drone in a day.
2. **COP31 ends with no deal** — After two days of overtime, the EU rejects a
   final draft that never mentions fossil fuels; the Antalya talks end with
   no agreement — only the third COP failure since 1995.
3. **Democrats clinch 258 House seats** — The largest House majority since
   1992: Chuck Hubbard beats Virginia Foxx by 330 votes after a recount,
   taking Democrats to 258 seats — 44 gained — with the Senate also in
   Democratic hands.

**More of the day**

4. FBI crime database hacked — the National Crime Information Center is
   breached; President Trump confirms the hack and the FBI investigates.
5. Trump backs a 10% cap on credit-card interest rates, working with Democrats.
6. Jakarta confectionery factory fire: 3 dead, 36 missing.
7. NATO vows to defend “every inch” of Polish territory after a Russian
   airspace violation; a cargo ship burns in the Baltic after a drone strike.
8. Sixth weekend of cost-of-living protests in France; thousands march in Lisbon.
9. Pentagon moves to curb defence ties with Canada; Ottawa says it sees no shift.
10. EU proposes a 3% tax on Big Tech, with €204bn of budget cuts.
11. 11 dead in Sinaloa after the capture of cartel boss “El Chapo Isidro”.
12. Boat capsizes on its way to a funeral in Nigeria: 24 dead.

**In brief:** South Korea's education chief resigns over an AI cheating
scandal; Hormuz may reopen if the US accepts “reasonable” terms, Iran says;
the Iran war has cost the US over $48bn (Pentagon); China opposes a US ban on
its telecom firms; states open a path to an accord on “killer robots”;
Belgium faces an air-traffic shutdown in Monday's national strike; Alcaraz
wins at the ATP Finals; Princess Catherine reflects on life after cancer in a
new film.
